*If I were to begin with no consideration for my readers, and in the way many (if not all) geoscience articles generally read, I would write something like the following as an introduction to my research.*

*For my PhD project, I am working in an alpine catchment called the Sulden catchment, which is located in the South Tyrol region of northern Italy.  In my ongoing research, I focus on the proglacial part of this catchment. A widely accepted definition of a proglacial system is that it is the part of a glacierized catchment bounded by the Little Ice Age (LIA) terminal moraine and the contemporary glacier boundary. However, there have been a lot of modifications in the Sulden catchment due to human activities, and the LIA terminal moraine is no longer visible. Hence, I assume the proglacial region of this catchment to be limited by the terminal moraine of the 1927 glacier advance. During the 1920s, many glaciers in the Alps had witnessed advances.*

# Catchment?

You can think of a catchment as a natural water-collecting system. Like a land, usually surrounded by mountains or hills, where rainfall flows downhill into streams and rivers that eventually drain out through a single outlet.

# Little Ice Age (LIA)?

Despite its dramatic name, the LIA was not a true ice age, but a period of regional cooling that lasted roughly from the 13th to the 19th century. Many parts of the world became noticeably cooler, leading to the expansion and advance of glaciers in several mountain regions.

---

# Moraine?

Imagine a mound of unsorted rocks and debris - that is what a moraine looks like. If you ask me, they are one of my favourite landforms to look at. They look fantastic in the field, and just as impressive in aerial photographs. Moraines are deposited by glaciers or ice sheets, which transport and leave behind all this material as they move and retreat.

Earlier, I used the term *terminal moraine*. Moraines can be classified into different types:

- A *terminal moraine* is a ridge of debris that marks the terminus, or end, of an advancing glacier. 
- A *medial moraine* forms where two glacier ice streams merge together.
- A *lateral moraine* forms along the sides of a glacier.

We have high peaks in the uplands, with elevations exceeding 3500 meters. You can also see the terminal moraine, which marks the former glacial extent in 1927.

Since then, the glacier has steadily receded, and this recession has become much more pronounced in recent years.

The LIA terminal moraine likely extended farther down-valley, but it is no longer preserved due to later human modifications of the landscape.
